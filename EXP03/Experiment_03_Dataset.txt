Experiment 3: Prompt Engineering using FLAN-T5-Small

Task 1: Explain a concept
Explain why a cricket batter should keep their head balanced while playing a straight drive.

Few-shot examples:
Input: Explain why a batter watches the ball.
Output: Watching the ball helps the batter judge its line and length and time the shot.
Input: Explain why footwork matters.
Output: Footwork helps the batter reach the ball while maintaining balance.

Task 2: Create a drill
Suggest a safe 10-minute beginner drill to practise front-foot defence.

Few-shot examples:
Input: Give a 5-minute warm-up.
Output: 1) Easy jogging, 2) shoulder mobility, 3) gentle dynamic stretches. Stop if pain occurs.
Input: Suggest a catching drill.
Output: Use soft throws, start close, focus on watching the ball, and increase distance gradually.

Task 3: Summarize
Summarize in two bullets: A batter should assess the line and length, move their feet early, keep their head steady, and practise consistently. A coach should give specific feedback and avoid changing too many things at once.

Few-shot examples:
Input: Summarize: Warm up first, practise slowly, then increase pace.
Output: Warm up before practice. Increase intensity gradually.
Input: Summarize: Watch the ball and keep balance.
Output: Track the ball. Maintain balance.

Task 4: Give feedback
A fictional batter repeatedly loses balance after attempting a cover drive. Give three constructive coaching suggestions without diagnosing an injury.

Few-shot examples:
Input: A batter swings too early.
Output: Track the ball longer. Practise slowly with controlled feeds. Ask a coach to observe timing.
Input: A batter's backlift is inconsistent.
Output: Use shadow practice. Repeat a comfortable setup. Review short video clips with a coach.

Task 5: Answer with uncertainty
Can a webcam alone prove that a cricket batter will score a century in their next match? Answer briefly and explain the limitation.

Few-shot examples:
Input: Can a video guarantee a player will never miss a shot?
Output: No. Video can show technique in the recorded conditions, but it cannot guarantee future performance.
Input: Can one practice session prove long-term improvement?
Output: No. Repeated measurements across sessions are needed.

Prompting techniques tested:
Zero-shot, Few-shot, Step-by-step, Role prompting.
